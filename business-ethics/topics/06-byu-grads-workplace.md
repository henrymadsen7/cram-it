# Topic 6: BYU Grads in the Workplace
## Source: Professor's review lecture (~40:00 - 48:00), Canvas Weeks 11-12

---

## The Core Challenge

You will enter a workplace with:
- People from very different backgrounds, beliefs, and lifestyles
- A need to be professionally effective and build influence
- Personal standards and values you do not want to compromise

The challenge: integrate socially enough to build the RELATIONAL CAPITAL you need to have ethical influence - without compromising your principles.

---

## Professionalism and Human Literacy

**Paul Lambert** (guest speaker): Religious diversity and pluralism are standard features of modern organizations.

"Human literacy" = understanding and navigating pluralism effectively
- Organizations bring people together to align around a common goal
- What HITS THE BRAKES on organizational synergy = lack of professionalism, lack of human literacy
- Your job: foster synergy, not impede it

---

## Building Relational Capital

**Why it matters for ethics**: You need relational capital to have ethical INFLUENCE.

"If you don't have relational capital with people, your ethical opinions and concerns will be ignored or dismissed."

Strategy: build trust BEFORE you need to use it.

The Ammon model (Book of Mormon):
- Ammon went in CURIOUS, wanting to SERVE
- Built relationships of trust first
- Then his beliefs and values naturally became of interest to others
- Contrast: Aaron went in direct, confrontational - less effective

## Practical Situations

### Happy Hour and Social Events
- You may not want to attend events where alcohol is served
- But social capital is built at these events
- Strategy: Show up for the first one (leverage primacy effects)
  - You'll be seen as someone willing to socialize with coworkers
  - Then you can miss some subsequent events without it affecting your reputation
  - "Show up for the first one and you're leveraging primacy effects to miss a few and still be seen as somebody who's willing to socialize"

### Interactions with Opposite-Sex Colleagues
- The professor mentioned there's a spectrum of approaches
- One extreme: never be alone with a member of the opposite sex
  - Risk: creates unfair access as a manager (some employees can't reach you)
- Other extreme: no boundaries, no transparency with spouse
  - Risk: can lead to serious problems
- Best practice: Counsel with your family FIRST. Get aligned on what boundaries work.
  - "The most important counsel is what happens in the family"
  - Set clear rules, maintain transparency, everyone is comfortable

### LGBTQ+ Colleagues and Different Lifestyles
- Professor's framework: WWJD (What Would Jesus Do?)
  - "The Savior's example is a marvelous and complete model"
  - He never forgot who he was or compromised his principles
  - Yet he was the best at compassion, inclusion, lifting those in the periphery
  - "I really believe asking that question will lead to inspired insight"
- Build common ground
- Show genuine interest in people as people

## The Core Principle

"Great clarity in principles, great charity in practice"

- Be very clear about what you believe and value (principles)
- Be very charitable and compassionate in how you interact with everyone (practice)

This is not compromise - it is sophisticated ethical living.

---

## Social Integration vs Differentiation

- Social integration = being part of the team, building relationships
- Differentiation = standing out in ways that reflect your values and standards

Employers (per BYU Employer Survey) expect both:
- They WANT employees who are professional, socially capable, collaborative
- They also WANT employees with strong character and values
- The job is to do both

---

## Fairness for All (Elder Rasband)

The reading argument:
- We can and should protect BOTH religious liberty AND LGBTQ+ protections
- These are not necessarily in zero-sum conflict
- Utah and Salt Lake were praised for legislation that protected both classes simultaneously
- "Other states were like, wow, that was innovative. Utah was figuring this out."
- The approach: find the overlapping space where both groups are protected, rather than playing them off against each other
