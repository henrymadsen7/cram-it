## Why Be Ethical? (Week 2)

False dichotomy: "I can be successful OR ethical" - this is wrong.

Why people behave ethically:
1. Psychological and physiological benefits (reduced stress, better health, sense of purpose)
2. Human nature (most people are naturally inclined toward cooperation)
3. Economic benefits - ethics REDUCES TRANSACTION COSTS
   - When people trust you, deals close faster, relationships are easier, overhead is lower
   - Unethical reputation is expensive: legal costs, damaged relationships, lost business
4. Building reputation for the long term

The economic model DOES NOT fully explain human behavior - people act ethically even when it costs them.

## The 4 Ethical Frameworks (Weeks 5-6) - KNOW ALL FOUR

### 1. Virtue Ethics
- Question: What kind of person does this make me? What would a person of good character do?
- Aristotle: cultivate virtues through habit and practice
- Strength: focuses on character, not just rules
- Application: "A truly honest person would..."
- Connection to humility: humility is a virtue that keeps other virtues calibrated

### 2. Utilitarianism
- Question: What produces the greatest good for the greatest number?
- Consequentialist: outcomes matter, not intent
- Strength: practical, considers real impact on real people
- Weakness: can justify harming a minority for the benefit of majority
- Application: "The decision that helps the most people is..."

### 3. Deontology (Kant)
- Question: What is my duty? What rule applies here?
- Rule-based: some actions are always right or wrong regardless of outcome
- "Never treat people as a means to an end"
- Strength: protects individual rights, consistent
- Weakness: can be rigid when rules conflict or don't cover a situation
- Application: "Regardless of the consequences, I have a duty to..."

### 4. Social Contract
- Question: What rules would rational people agree to in order to live together?
- Rawls' "veil of ignorance": design rules not knowing what position you'll be in
- Strength: democratic, consensus-based
- Application: "A fair rule that any member of society could agree to would be..."

## Kohlberg's Levels of Moral Development (Week 6)

### Pre-Conventional (Self-Interest)
- Stage 1: Avoid punishment (do right to avoid getting caught)
- Stage 2: Seek reward (do right when it benefits me)

### Conventional (Social Norms)
- Stage 3: Good relationships (do right to be seen as a good person by others)
- Stage 4: Law and order (do right because rules say so, uphold society's rules)

### Post-Conventional (Principles)
- Stage 5: Social contract (do right because it reflects agreements reasonable people would make)
- Stage 6: Universal principles (do right because of deep ethical principles, even if laws say otherwise)

Goal: operate at Stages 5-6. Understand what stage you (and others) are operating at.

## Self-Awareness and Ethical Identity (Week 4)

**Revisionary ethics**: tendency to revise our memory of past decisions to make ourselves look more ethical than we were

**Forecasting errors**: misjudging how we will behave in future ethical situations ("I would never do that" - but then you do)

**Self-Growth Model**: framework for deliberate character development
- Awareness -> Reflection -> Action -> Habit -> Character

**Covey's Quadrants**: urgent/important matrix - applied to ethical decisions means: don't let urgency crowd out what's truly important

**Hyperbolic discounting**: tendency to overvalue immediate rewards vs future consequences. This is why people make bad short-term ethical choices.

**Virtue circumplex**: framework mapping out virtues and their relationships to each other

## Moral Regulation (Week 6-7)

**Moral licensing**: "I've been so good lately, I can afford to slip a little"
- Past good behavior creates psychological permission to do wrong
- PROBLEM: ethical behavior should not be treated as a deposit you can withdraw

**Moral cleansing**: doing good to offset a recent wrong
- Can be healthy if it leads to genuine improvement
- Can be problematic if it's just "buying indulgences" with no real change

**Prescriptive ethics**: proactively doing good (affirmative)
**Proscriptive ethics**: avoiding doing wrong (prohibitive)

Professor's recommendation: shift more focus to PRESCRIPTIVE ethics. Don't just avoid bad - actively pursue good.

## Moral Rationalization (Week 8)

Tactics people use to justify unethical behavior to themselves:

| Tactic | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Denial of responsibility | "I was just following orders" |
| Denial of injury | "Nobody was really hurt" |
| Denial of victim | "They deserved it" |
| Appeal to higher loyalties | "I did it for my family / my team" |
| Condemnation of condemners | "Everyone does it, who are you to judge?" |
| Displacement of responsibility | "It wasn't really my decision" |

Countermeasure: have prepared counter-statements or questions for the rationalizations you are most susceptible to.

## CSR - Corporate Social Responsibility (Week 15)

CSR = when companies take responsibility for their impact on society beyond what is legally required

Why companies engage:
- Ethical obligation to stakeholders
- Business case: reputation, employee loyalty, long-term sustainability
- Stakeholder pressure

Why CSR can backfire:
- Performative / PR-driven CSR destroys trust when seen through
- Wasteful CSR draws from shareholder value without genuine benefit
- "Robbing from shareholders" argument (Milton Friedman-adjacent)

Strong CSR = genuine, efficient, tied to core business, proven impact

Professor quote: "Go Forth to Serve - plan to make service a lifelong pursuit and source of joy"
