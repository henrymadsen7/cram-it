## Why Diversity Matters

Diversity in organizations adds:
- Creativity (different perspectives generate new ideas)
- Specialization (different backgrounds bring different expertise)
- Nuanced perspectives
- Better decision-making (groups with diversity catch more blind spots)

BYU acknowledges this and invests resources in increasing diversity on campus. The professor noted the "needle is not moving as fast as we want, but it is moving."

---

## Key Distinction: Diversity vs Affirmative Action

These are NOT the same thing.

**Diversity initiative**:
- Voluntary, broad efforts to recruit, retain, and include people from varied backgrounds
- Ongoing, not time-limited
- Not legally required in the same way
- Examples: targeted recruiting events, inclusive culture programs, mentorship pipelines

**Affirmative action**:
- Legally specific program
- Must meet strict legal requirements to be lawful
- Has specific rules about when, where, and how it can be used
- More controversial and legally constrained

## The Legal Status (KNOW THIS - likely a T/F question)

### "Affirmative action is unconstitutional" - TRUE or FALSE?

**ANSWER: Generally FALSE**, but heavily constrained.

The Supreme Court still allows a very constrained form, BUT:

### Education (Universities)
- **Race-conscious admissions = BANNED** (Supreme Court ruled against Harvard, University of Michigan)
- You CANNOT use race as a specific numeric factor (e.g., adding points for race, quota systems)
- You CAN consider race IF the applicant explains in their essay how their background/experiences with discrimination added to who they are and could add sociological value to the university - case-by-case only

### Employment (Workplace)
- Affirmative action plans = STILL LEGAL in most states
- BANNED in: California, Michigan, Washington
- Where legal, it must meet all of these requirements:
  1. **Written down** - must be a concrete, formal plan
  2. **Proves historical disparity** - must demonstrate an underrepresented group has been historically excluded
  3. **Must end when the disparity is rectified** - it's TEMPORARY, not permanent
  4. Cannot be perpetuated once the goal is achieved

## Human Capital Decisions

Human capital decisions (hiring, promotions, admissions) are:
- The decisions most likely to result in lawsuits
- Legally the most complex area of management

**Professor's advice**: Before making these decisions:
1. Know the applicable laws
2. Take HR or Legal out to lunch - ask who has gotten in trouble and why
3. Ask for a primer or quick training on what to be careful about
4. When in doubt: consult HR/legal before deciding

"The chance of lawsuits is much higher with human capital related decisions."

## The BYU Admissions Case (Group Decision-Making)

The case was used to illustrate:
- **Groupthink**: when groups avoid conflict and reach consensus too quickly without critical evaluation
- **Groupshift**: groups tend to make more extreme decisions than individuals alone
- **Thresholds**: everyone has a different threshold for what they consider "enough" diversity/merit/qualification
- **Values-based decision-making**: admissions decisions force you to explicitly identify what you value

In the case discussion:
- Identify which values are in tension
- Notice how group dynamics affect the decision
- See the difference between diversity initiatives and affirmative action in real decisions

## Connection to Stakeholder Theory

When making human capital decisions, who are your stakeholders?
- The individual candidate (has urgency + legitimacy)
- Other candidates (fairness to all applicants)
- The organization (needs the best qualified person)
- Society (interest in reducing historical disparities)

A stakeholder analysis can help frame why diversity matters beyond just legal compliance.
