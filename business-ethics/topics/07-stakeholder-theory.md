## The Problem Stakeholder Theory Solves

Companies have MANY parties that care about what they do. Who should you actually pay attention to?

Early stakeholder theory just said: pay attention to everyone beyond shareholders.
Problem: too unwieldy. Companies threw up their hands and went back to "maximize shareholder value only."

Milton Friedman's counter-argument: "The social responsibility of business is to increase its profits" - serve shareholders only.

Problem with that: leads to myopic, short-term thinking that ignores real consequences.

The Salience Model is the practical SOLUTION to this standoff.

---

## Primary vs Secondary Stakeholders

**Primary stakeholders** = have formal contractual relationships with the company
- Examples: employees, shareholders, customers, suppliers

**Secondary stakeholders** = no formal contract, but affected by company's actions
- Examples: local community, environment, future generations, NGOs
- Key point: secondary stakeholders are often IGNORED but can come back in force with lawsuits and liability

---

## The Stakeholder Salience Model (Mitchell, Agle, Wood)

### The 3 Dimensions

**POWER**
- Can this stakeholder affect the company?
- Do they have resources, leverage, ability to harm or help?
- Relatively straightforward

**LEGITIMACY**
- Do they have a moral/ethical claim on the company?
- Is there a commonly agreed-upon moral connection between what the company does and this stakeholder's interests?
- More nuanced than power - it is MORAL legitimacy, not just legal
- Example: a community near a polluting factory has legitimacy because there is a clear moral connection between the company's actions and their health

**URGENCY**
- Is their claim time-sensitive or critical?
- Is there time pressure or high importance to their demand?
- Relatively straightforward

---

## THE VENN DIAGRAM (Memorize This)

Three overlapping circles: P (Power), L (Legitimacy), U (Urgency)

Overlap categories:

| Attributes Present | Category | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| P + L + U (all three) | DEFINITIVE | HIGHEST - must address |
| P + L only | DOMINANT | Important |
| L + U only | DEPENDENT | Need an advocate |
| P + U only | DANGEROUS | Watch carefully |
| P only | DORMANT | Monitor |
| L only | DISCRETIONARY | Low priority |
| U only | DEMANDING | Low priority |
| None | NON-STAKEHOLDER | Not your concern |

### The Key Insight
This model tells you WHO IS IN and WHO IS OUT.
The previous version of stakeholder theory didn't give you this clarity.

## How to Apply (Exam Skill)

Case: Tesla self-driving cars - who do you invite to help make decisions?

Step 1: List all potential stakeholders
Step 2: For each one, ask: do they have Power? Legitimacy? Urgency?
Step 3: Assign to a category
Step 4: Prioritize your attention accordingly

Definitive stakeholders = address their concerns immediately
Dominant/Dependent/Dangerous = keep on your radar
Latent (one attribute only) = monitor, don't ignore
Non-stakeholders = no obligation currently

---

## Internal and External Uses of the Model

**Internal use** (if you're inside the org):
- Categorize stakeholders to know who makes it to the top of your agenda
- Who with low power/legitimacy/urgency should still be watched?

**External use** (if you're the stakeholder):
- How can you BECOME a more central concern for the company?
- What do you need to gain to move from Dependent to Definitive?
- Building power, establishing legitimacy, creating urgency

## Pros and Cons of Stakeholder Theory

### Pros
- Promotes long-term thinking over short-term
- Encourages CSR (corporate social responsibility)
- Requires acknowledgment of externalities
- Better strategy (avoids blindsided lawsuits from neglected secondary stakeholders)

### Cons
- Makes decision-making SLOWER (more stakeholders = more check-ins)
- Can pressure companies into wasteful or performative charitable activities
- Milton Friedman argument: you're spending shareholders' money on things they didn't authorize

---

## Connection to CSR

Stakeholder theory naturally leads to CSR.
CSR works when it's genuine and efficient.
CSR backfires when it's performative, wasteful, or just PR.

Costco example: prof mentioned as genuine stakeholder care model.
