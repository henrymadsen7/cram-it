# Topic 2: Commodification of Society
## Source: Professor's review lecture (~14:00 - 20:00), Canvas Week 9

---

## The Core Idea

Market economy = using market mechanisms for goods and services (normal, fine)
Market society = applying market logic to EVERYTHING, including things that shouldn't be priced

The problem: when the metaphor of the market is inappropriately overlaid onto everything.

"Praise is the money that makes the world go around" = example of market-society thinking.

---

## The Key Question

When is it appropriate (or not) to commoditize a good or service?

This is the skill you need for the exam. You need to be able to analyze a case using the 5 tests.

---

## The 5 Tests for Commodification

Use these to evaluate ANY good or service:

### Test 1: Fairness / Equality
Does commoditizing this good unfairly impact certain groups of people?
Does it create or reinforce inequality?

### Test 2: Coercion / Rights
Does offering money for this good or service coerce people?
Does it violate anyone's rights or exploit people in desperate situations?

### Test 3: Corruption
Does turning this into a commodity corrupt or degrade something that should be valued differently?
Does it treat something with a LOWER mode of valuation than is appropriate?
(Money is not the right mode of valuation for everything)

### Test 4: Social Value
Does commoditizing this create or destroy social value?
What happens to society when this is turned into a market transaction?

### Test 5: Proper Mode of Valuation
Is money the right way to relate to this good/service?
Some things have a proper mode of valuation that money violates (love, dignity, life)

## Case Studies (know these)

### Disneyland Disabled-Person Line Skipping
- Rich families hired disabled people to pose as family members to skip lines
- Raises: corruption (dignity), fairness (access based on wealth not need), mode of valuation

### Project Prevention (Barbara Harris)
- Organization paying drug-addicted and convicted mothers money to undergo permanent sterilization
- Intent: prevent babies being born into drug-addicted environments
- Ethical issues:
  - Corruption test: Are we treating the capacity to have children as a commodity?
  - Coercion test: Is offering money to desperate people truly voluntary?
  - Professor note: Harris has since offered temporary birth control options too
- Heavy emphasis on CORRUPTION component in lecture

### Police Car Advertisements
- Selling ad space on police car decals
- Issues: impartiality (does this create conflicts of interest for police?)
- Dignity of office (does advertising reduce respect for law enforcement?)

### Ford Pinto / Dark Side of Goal Setting
- Ford was first company charged with homicide as an organization
- Recall coordinator was severely overworked and under-resourced
- Lesson 1: for critical safety decisions, ensure ADEQUATE human capital
- Lesson 2: Goal setting has a dark side - can create perverse incentives

### Wells Fargo (Perverse Incentive)
- Corporate mandate: sign up X customers for plus accounts and credit cards or be fired
- Local branches: to avoid being fired, signed customers up WITHOUT their knowledge
- Never sent the credit card, just made the quota look good
- Created fraudulent accounts for millions of customers
- A very reputable bank destroyed by bad goal design
- Lesson: unintended consequences of aggressive performance targets

## How to Apply on the Exam

If the exam asks: "Should Company X sell/commoditize Y?"

Walk through the 5 tests:
1. Is this fair to all groups? Does wealth/power determine access?
2. Is anyone being coerced or exploited?
3. Does pricing this corrupt or degrade something inherently valuable?
4. What happens to society if this becomes a normal market transaction?
5. Is money the right medium for this relationship?

If multiple tests trigger = the good/service should NOT be commoditized (or should be heavily regulated).
