# Topic 1: Leader Humility
## Source: Professor's review lecture (minutes 0:00 - 14:00), Canvas Week 9

---

## The Core Idea

Humility is not weakness - it is a LENS that enables you to see more completely, accurately, and appreciatively. The professor's research identified it as a direct response to the "crisis of leadership" and large-scale business scandals (Enron, etc.).

Traditional leadership traits (vision, decisiveness, competence) are still essential. Humility TEMPERS and AMPLIFIES them.

---

## The 3 Dimensions (Developmental Sequence)

These build on each other - it's a spiral:

### Stage 1: Self-Awareness (Illumination)
- Become aware of both strengths AND limitations
- Humus/humility literally means "from the ground" - awareness prepares the soil
- Uncomfortable but necessary before growth
- This is the foundation

### Stage 2: Teachability
- Being grounded = able to receive wisdom, feedback, inspiration
- People with humility GLEAN more from everyday experiences
- Evidence: at West Point, teachability literally saved lives
- Steve Jobs 2.0 was teachable; Steve Jobs 1.0 was not

### Stage 3: Appreciation of Others / Seeking Mentors
- Seeing others' strengths without feeling threatened
- Micro-mentors: people who excel at one specific thing you can learn from
  - Not everyone has access to macro-mentors (expensive coaches, senior executives)
  - Micro-mentors are all around you if you have the humility lens
- Humility lets you see others as exemplars, not competitors

## The Accurate Sense of Self

The professor's model measures self on two axes: **Integrity** and **Ability**

Think of it as a circle:
- Space INSIDE the circle = ground you've already gained
- Space OUTSIDE = your potential for growth

### Inflated Sense of Self
- Circle is drawn TOO LARGE (exaggerated view of your ability and integrity)
- Consequences: overclaims, doesn't seek feedback, is tender/defensive when challenged
- Like a swollen ankle - you poke it and they react with pain
- Example: Andy Fastow (Enron CFO) - won CFO of the Year, then went to prison
  - His praised ability made him feel moral legitimacy he didn't actually have
- Conflating exceptional ABILITY with exceptional INTEGRITY is the trap

### Compressed Sense of Self
- Circle drawn TOO SMALL (underestimating your own strengths)
- Also a problem - not humility, it's a different kind of self-deception
- Prevents you from doing the good you could do (Back to the Future reference)
- Not quite as morally risky, but still impedes ethical action

### Virtuous Humility
- Accurately sized circle
- Enables self-affirmation (acknowledging strengths) AND acknowledgement of growth areas

## Key Case Studies

### Steve Jobs (Tempered Narcissist)
- Jobs 1.0: brilliant, but caustic and toxic. Was LIQUIDATING value through behavior
- Got fired from Apple - humbling experience
- Jobs 2.0: still had drive/vision/narcissism, but it was TEMPERED
  - More likely to acknowledge past mistakes
  - More able to identify and credit others' great ideas
  - Built team, built trust
  - Built Apple into the most valuable company in the world
- Lesson: you don't have to eliminate ambition - just temper it with humility

### Andy Fastow (Enron)
- CFO of the Year Award -> prison ID card
- Exceptional ability + praise -> inflated sense of moral legitimacy
- "He felt like he could do no wrong"

### Titanic vs Olympic
- Titanic = pride and arrogance; didn't slow down, didn't heed warnings
- Olympic (sister ship) = captained with humility mindset
- Question for your life: Are you captaining with Olympic humility or Titanic arrogance?

## Tools and Tactics

### TAGS List (for decompression of compressed sense of self)
- T = Talents
- A = Achievements
- G = Goals
- S = Service
- Purpose: build a realistic, comprehensive picture of who you are
- Includes both past-oriented AND future-oriented elements
- Leads to self-affirmation = the confidence needed to then acknowledge limitations

### SWOT Analysis (for deflation of inflated sense of self)
- Forces acknowledgement of both strengths (self-affirmation) AND weaknesses/threats
- Helps the inflated person face reality in a structured, non-threatening way

---

## How Humility Connects to Ethics

- Humility is a TEMPERANCE VIRTUE - keeps other virtues in the Aristotelian "golden mean"
- Courage without humility becomes recklessness
- Confidence without humility becomes arrogance
- Humility keeps everything calibrated

Without humility:
- You miss information (don't seek feedback)
- You don't learn from others
- You believe your past success justifies future ethical shortcuts
- You become the Titanic
