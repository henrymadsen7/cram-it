# Topic 8: Conflict of Interest
## Source: Professor's review lecture (~58:00 - 61:00), Canvas Week 14

---

## The Core Rule

**ALWAYS DISCLOSE when you have a conflict of interest.**

"That should be the default nature of reaction when you perceive it."

---

## What Is a Conflict of Interest?

A conflict of interest occurs when:
- You have been hired/given responsibility to make decisions in the BEST INTEREST OF THE ORGANIZATION
- But another role you hold may COMPROMISE YOUR OBJECTIVITY in making those decisions

The conflict is between:
- Role A: organizational decision-maker (what you're paid to do)
- Role B: personal relationship, family tie, financial interest, etc.

---

## Common Types

**Nepotism** = favoring a family member in hiring/promotion decisions
- Your role as family member pulls against your role as objective manager
- The family member may not be the best candidate, but the relationship pressures you

**Financial interest** = owning stock in a company you're also evaluating, advising, or regulating

**Personal relationships** = reviewing a paper/grant from someone you know, have worked with, or have a prior relationship with

**Prior commitments** = making decisions that benefit a previous employer or partner

---

## The Protocol

### Step 1: DISCLOSE
As soon as you perceive that another role may compromise your objectivity, disclose it.
- Tell your supervisor, the relevant parties, or document it formally
- "This situation may create a conflict of interest for me because..."

### Step 2: WITHDRAW if necessary
If disclosure alone isn't sufficient to resolve the conflict, step back from the decision entirely.
- Recuse yourself
- Let someone without the conflict make the call

### Professor's example:
He was asked to review a paper by scholars who were part of his own Templeton Foundation grant. He recognized this as a conflict of interest and withdrew from reviewing it - even though technically he was just reviewing a paper. The granting institution would not have been happy with the appearance of impropriety.

---

## Why People Get Burned

The professor noted that people who have completed business school training STILL get into trouble with conflicts of interest.

Why?
- They don't perceive the conflict
- They think "I can be objective anyway"
- They underestimate how the appearance of conflict affects trust
- They don't disclose because they're embarrassed or don't want to lose the opportunity

The ethical responsibility is:
1. Perceive that the conflict exists
2. Disclose it even when it feels awkward

---

## Connection to Stakeholder Theory

When you have a conflict of interest, you are essentially giving one stakeholder (yourself, your family) priority over stakeholders who should have equal or higher priority (the organization, its clients, the public).

Proper stakeholder management requires managing conflicts of interest.

## Likely Test Questions

2. "Define conflict of interest and give an example" - two roles in tension, compromising objectivity
3. "What are the steps when you have a conflict of interest?" - disclose, then withdraw
4. Short scenario: you're asked to evaluate a vendor you have a financial relationship with - what do you do?
5. "Why is nepotism a conflict of interest?" - family role vs organizational decision-making role
